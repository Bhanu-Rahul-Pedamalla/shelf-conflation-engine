Realogram · what the badge reads

1The problem

Retailers don't know what is on their shelves right now.

"I couldn't believe none of the retailers had any idea what was on their shelves." Ross Finman, who went on to found Augmodo, after hunting for baby formula in the 2022 shortage

$1.75 trillion a year lost to out-of-stocks, overstocks and returns IHL Group, the figure Augmodo cites

2Augmodo's answer

A camera badge on workers already walking.

So Augmodo, a Seattle company, put a camera badge on the people who already walk the aisles.

Dozens of scans of every shelf a day Augmodo

100× cheaper than the other way, a shelf-scanning robot Augmodo

Every walk becomes a scan. The result is what Augmodo calls the Realogram: a live map of the product in every shelf spot.

Below is one aisle of it, as the badge sees it: one shelf spot at a time, and the text the badge read off it. Under each read, a simple lookup tries to name the product in the retailer's list: green when it can, amber when it cannot, red when it names the wrong one. The shelf from act 1 runs along the bottom; the figure is the worker walking it.

Click or tap any shelf spot to hold it and see every read of it. The walk keeps going.

Front row only; a product pushed behind another is found by a worker, not a camera.

So why not ask an LLM?

It would name this one. And the next. And the bill rises with every walk, forever.

So you name each read as cheaply as it can be named.

Rules first. Fuzzy next. An LLM last, only for what nothing else can name. One trusted record per shelf spot, kept right as the catalog changes.
