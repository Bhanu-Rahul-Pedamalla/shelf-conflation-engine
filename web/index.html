Realogram · what the badge reads

  1. The problem

    Retailers do not know what is on their shelves right now: empty spots, wrong products, promotions not set up. Chemist Warehouse's CIO said his associates used to spend hours auditing.

  2. Augmodo's answer

    A camera badge on workers already walking the aisles; every walk becomes a scan. Augmodo says it is 100 times cheaper than a shelf robot. The Realogram: a live map of the front-facing product in every shelf spot, updated dozens of times a day. Front-facing only: a product hidden behind another stays invisible until a worker finds it.

  3. The problem inside it

    The badge reads text, not products: . The retailer's catalog is a separate, messy list. Until each read is tied to one catalog row, the map is a guess. Augmodo says it processes hundreds of terabytes a day.

  4. The obvious fix, and why not

    Send every read to an LLM. It would name most of them. Its cost grows with every read, and the reads grow with every walk.

  5. The job

    Rules and regex first, cheap and exact. Fuzzy matching with blocking next. An LLM only for the few reads nothing else can name, its confidence capped. One trusted record per shelf spot, versioned as the catalog changes. This simulation is the input to that job.

The full store

  • The badge is a small camera worn by a store associate. As they walk past a shelf it reads the label of every product facing, and sometimes its barcode.
